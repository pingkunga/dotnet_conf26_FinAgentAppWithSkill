# Where to keep it

An emergency fund has three competing priorities, in this order:

1. **Liquidity** — accessible within a day or two, with no penalty for withdrawing early. This rules out
   anything that locks money up for a fixed term or charges an early-withdrawal fee.
2. **Safety** — the balance shouldn't drop in value right when it might be needed most. This generally rules
   out anything whose value can meaningfully swing day to day (equities, most crypto).
3. **Yield** — a nice-to-have once the first two are satisfied, not a reason to compromise on either.

In practice this usually points to a savings-type account that's separate from everyday spending money (so
it isn't accidentally spent) but still reachable without delay. Don't recommend specific banks, brokers, or
named products — describe the *properties* to look for (FDIC/deposit-insurance-equivalent coverage where
applicable, no lock-in period, no or low minimum-balance penalties) and let the user match those properties
to what's available to them, since specific institutions and rates vary by country and change over time.

A common mistake is treating "highest interest rate" as the deciding factor — for an emergency fund,
liquidity and safety should never be traded away for a marginally better rate.
