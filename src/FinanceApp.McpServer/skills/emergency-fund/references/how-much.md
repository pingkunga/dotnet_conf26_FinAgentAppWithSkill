# How much is enough?

A common starting point is **3 to 6 months of essential expenses** — rent/mortgage, utilities, food,
insurance, minimum debt payments — not total income and not discretionary spending.

Factors that push toward the **higher** end (6+ months):

- Income is variable or commission-based, or comes from a single household earner
- The job/industry is cyclical or currently unstable
- Dependents (children, an aging parent) who rely on that income
- No other quick-access safety net (family help, short-term disability coverage)

Factors that can justify the **lower** end (3 months, sometimes less while building toward a bigger goal):

- Stable dual-income household with overlapping skill sets (either earner could likely re-employ quickly)
- Strong employer benefits (severance policy, long unemployment-insurance eligibility)
- Very low fixed monthly expenses relative to income

There's no single universally-correct number — the point of a range is that "3 months" and "6 months" are
both defensible answers depending on the person's actual stability, not that one is right and the other
wrong. When advising, ask what drives the uncertainty in their situation rather than asserting a single
target number as fact.
